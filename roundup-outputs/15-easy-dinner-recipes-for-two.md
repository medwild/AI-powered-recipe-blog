# 15 Easy Dinner Recipes for Two – Quick, Cozy Meals from My Kitchen

When you’re cooking for two, the right recipe feels like a kitchen shortcut that still delivers a full, satisfying dinner. This collection is exactly that: 15 tested, trusted recipes from my own kitchen, each designed to feed two people without a mountain of leftovers or a sink full of pans. Whether you need a 20-minute pasta on a busy Tuesday or a comforting chicken pot pie for a cozy weekend, these dishes cover the range. Every recipe is written with small-batch portions, straightforward steps, and real-world timing in mind. In my kitchen, these are the ones I return to again and again — because they just work.

## Why These Recipes Work for Two

What makes a recipe truly work for two people? It’s not just about halving a larger recipe. A well-scaled dinner for two means the ingredient quantities make sense — no half an onion languishing in the fridge, no leftover sauce that you’ll forget about. It means the cooking times are adjusted for smaller pans and smaller volumes, so your chicken sears without steaming and your pasta water boils fast. It also means one-pan or one-pot methods whenever possible, because a romantic dinner shouldn’t leave you with a pile of dishes. In this roundup, you’ll find recipes that lean heavily on skillets, sheet pans, and a single pot. Many pull double duty: the same pan that sears the protein also builds the pan sauce. Portions are generous but not excessive, so you’re not eating the same meal for four days straight. These are the principles I cook by, and they’re baked into every recipe below.

## How Long Do These Dinner Recipes Take?

The 15 recipes here range from 20 minutes to just under an hour, with most falling in the 25- to 35-minute sweet spot. The quickest is the 20-minute garlic butter chicken pasta, a weeknight hero when you need dinner on the table fast. The 25-minute recipes — like the pan-seared chicken with herb butter, stovetop mac and cheese, beef ramen noodles, and garlic butter chicken bites — are all built around high-heat searing and fast pan sauces. The 30-minute offerings (garlic butter shrimp and salmon orzo) feel a little more special but still keep things moving. At 35 minutes, you’ll find one-pan meals like the chicken enchilada skillet and garlic tomato chicken and rice that do a bit of simmering. The 45-minute recipes, including chili colorado and chicken pot pie, are weekend-friendly comfort food that rewards a little extra time. The lasagna takes 55 minutes but is mostly hands-off oven time. No matter which you pick, the active cooking time is manageable, and the rhythm of each recipe is designed for a relaxed evening.

### What supplies do I need for these one-pan recipes?

Most of these recipes call for a 10- or 12-inch skillet, preferably one that can go from stovetop to oven if needed. A good chef’s knife and a cutting board will handle the prep. Beyond that, you’ll just need basic pots for boiling pasta or rice, a baking sheet for the pot pie or lasagna, and a few mixing bowls. I cook with simple, everyday tools, and these recipes reflect that.

### Can I adjust these recipes if I’m cooking for one?

Absolutely. The portions are designed for two, but halving them is straightforward. For recipes that use a single chicken breast or steak, you can cook one and save the second piece for another meal. For skillet dishes with rice or pasta, you can cut the ingredients in half and reduce the cooking time slightly. The recipe notes often include tips for scaling down.

### Are there vegetarian options in this list?

While the majority of these recipes feature meat or seafood, the stovetop mac and cheese is vegetarian and can be a satisfying main dish when paired with a side salad. You could also adapt the one-pan garlic tomato chicken and rice by swapping the chicken for chickpeas and using vegetable broth. The garlic butter shrimp could be replaced with pan-seared mushrooms for a similar texture.

### How do I store leftovers for two?

I recommend storing leftovers in airtight containers in the fridge. Most of these dishes will keep for up to three days. For recipes with crispy elements like the puff pastry lid on the pot pie, it’s best to reheat in the oven to restore some texture. The chili colorado, lasagna, and chicken skillet dishes often taste even better the next day. I try to portion them into two-serving containers so you have a ready-made lunch.

### What’s the best way to time multiple components of a dinner?

I always read the recipe through first, then gather all my ingredients before I start cooking. Many of these recipes are written to use the same pan in stages, so you’ll sear a protein, set it aside, and build the sauce while the pasta or rice cooks. I’ll often start the side that takes the longest (like rice or roasted vegetables) first, then move to the skillet work. With practice, you’ll find a rhythm that makes the whole meal come together at the same time.

### Can I make these recipes ahead of time?

Several of these dinners work well as make-ahead meals. The chicken enchilada skillet, chili colorado, and lasagna can be assembled or fully cooked the day before and reheated. The garlic butter chicken pasta is best fresh, but you can prep the chicken and measure out ingredients ahead of time. For the salmon orzo, I’d recommend cooking it just before serving, as the orzo can absorb liquid and soften further upon sitting.
