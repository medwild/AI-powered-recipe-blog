## Complete Recipe Analysis — Half Baked Harvest (Tieghan Gerard)

**Recipe**: Skillet Burst Cherry Tomato White Wine Garlic Pasta
**Source**: halfbakedharvest.com
**Why this blog**: 13+ years of authentic human content. Distinct visual and narrative voice. Never uses AI.

---

### Section 1: Opening — Title as First Sentence

> "Skillet Burst Cherry Tomato White Wine Garlic Pasta. Sweet cherry tomatoes are blistered with butter, garlic, fresh herbs, and a splash of white wine, then tossed with bucatini and creamy burrata. Finished with homemade lemon garlic breadcrumbs, it's an easy summer pasta that's perfect for nights when you're craving Italian flavors made with the season's freshest ingredients."

**Why it works:**
- The title IS the first sentence. No separation.
- 3 action verbs cascade: blistered → tossed → finished
- "A splash of white wine" — sensory, not measured. An LLM writes "60ml dry white wine"

---

### Section 2: Personal Hook

> "Aside from summer salads, this is the kind of dinner I crave most this time of year. The simplest pasta made with what's fresh and growing—ingredients that are practically at our fingertips if you're lucky enough to have a little summer garden."

**Why it works:**
- "I crave" — personal desire, not generic recommendation
- "if you're lucky enough" — conditional empathy. Admits not everyone has a garden
- "little summer garden" — "little" is the key word. Modest, not aspirational

---

### Section 3: Childhood Memory — The Unfakeable Detail

> "I talk about my childhood a lot because so many of my favorite recipes come from watching my dad cook during our Ohio summers. Pasta was always on the dinner table at least once a week, and as soon as my dad's tomato plants were overflowing, those tomatoes found their way into simple salads and quick stovetop pastas. There was always garlic involved (the jar of pre-chopped garlic was his favorite), plenty of fresh basil, a generous handful of cheese, cracked black pepper, and, of course, chili flakes. My dad never held back on the olive oil, the cheese, or the chili flakes. He loves a saucy pasta and believes almost everything tastes better with a little heat."

**The unfakeable details:**
| Detail | Why LLMs can't invent this |
|---|---|
| "(the jar of pre-chopped garlic was his favorite)" | A chef would hide this. She shares it with love. It's real. |
| "Ohio summers" | A real place, not "the Italian countryside" or "growing up in a kitchen" |
| The ternary rhythm | "olive oil, the cheese, or the chili flakes" — repeated twice. Musical. |
| Present tense love | "He loves a saucy pasta" — her dad is alive, still cooking. Not fabricated nostalgia. |

---

### Section 4: Bridge — Memory to Recipe

> "Those simple summer dinners are still some of my favorites, and they're exactly what I thought about when I first made this recipe. A few weeks ago, after getting home from a work trip, I was determined not to run to the grocery store or place an Instacart order. I looked around the kitchen and realized I already had everything I needed, right down to a loaf of ciabatta tucked away in the freezer. It felt like the perfect excuse to make the simplest summer pasta."

**Why it works:**
- "after getting home from a work trip" — mundane real life. Not "returning from Tuscany"
- "Instacart" — brand name. Makes it contemporary and concrete
- "ciabatta tucked away in the freezer" — precise image. You can SEE it

---

### Section 5: Cooking Philosophy

> "When it comes to summer cooking, I've always believed simple is best. If I don't have to turn on the oven, even better. Pour a glass of wine, let the tomatoes do the work, and call it dinner."

**Why it works:**
- "If I don't have to turn on the oven, even better" — anti-effort principle. Human laziness as virtue
- "call it dinner" — oral expression. Zero pretension. Abrupt, confident ending

---

### Section 6: Tieghan's Tip — The Signature Pattern

> "Tieghan's Tip: If you love garlic and cheese as much as I do, grate a clove of fresh garlic into the warm breadcrumbs right after cooking, then toss with a handful of grated parmesan. The residual heat softens the garlic just enough, and the parmesan makes the breadcrumbs even more irresistible."

**Why this is the strongest human signal in the entire text:**
| Element | Effect |
|---|---|
| Named signature | "Tieghan's Tip:" — she signs her advice. It's HER. |
| Complicity | "If you love garlic and cheese as much as I do" — creates an in-group |
| Causal mechanism | "The residual heat softens the garlic" — explains WHY, not just WHAT |
| Bonus, not instruction | It's not in the formal recipe. It's an extra, a secret shared |

---

### Section 7: Substitution with Reassurance

> "Recipe Note: If you don't typically cook with wine or don't have a bottle on hand, simply substitute water or broth. Add a squeeze of fresh lemon juice for brightness or stir in a teaspoon of apple cider vinegar for a little extra depth and acidity. It'll still be just as delicious."

**Why it works:**
- Suggests TWO compensation methods (lemon juice OR apple cider vinegar)
- Explains what each one adds (brightness vs depth/acidity)
- Ends with reassurance: "It'll still be just as delicious"

---

### Section 8: Instructions with Interruption

> "These breadcrumbs are one of my favorite parts of the recipe. They add the best crunch and make a simple pasta feel just a little more elevated."

> "That starchy pasta water is the secret to a silky, restaurant-style sauce."

> "As the tomatoes burst, they naturally create the sauce. The butter and white wine make it rich without feeling heavy, which is exactly what I love during the summer."

> "Don't rush this step. Tossing the pasta for an extra minute helps the sauce cling to every noodle."

**Patterns used between steps:**
| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| Personal commentary | "These breadcrumbs are one of my favorite parts" |
| Standalone wisdom line | "That starchy pasta water is the secret to a silky, restaurant-style sauce." |
| "I love" mid-instruction | "which is exactly what I love during the summer" |
| Imperative + science | "Don't rush this step. Tossing...helps the sauce cling to every noodle" |

---

### Section 9: Closing — Scene, Not Instruction

> "This is one of my favorite dinners for entertaining because everything comes together so easily. Set the serving bowl in the middle of the table with extra basil, flaky sea salt, and the remaining breadcrumbs so everyone can finish their own bowl."

> "When tomatoes are at their peak, they really don't need much else!"

**Why it works:**
- Paints a table scene: "Set the serving bowl in the middle of the table"
- Gives guests autonomy: "everyone can finish their own bowl"
- Closes with enthusiasm, not command: "they really don't need much else!"
- NOWHERE does it say "Enjoy!" or "Serve immediately"

## The 7 Universal Patterns (Extracted from Complete Analysis)

These patterns appear in ALL human-written food blogs and NEVER in AI-generated content:

1. **Title = First Sentence** — No gap between headline and body
2. **Parenthetical Asides** — Details in (parentheses) that feel off-script and personal
3. **Named Tips** — "[Author]'s Tip:" with causal mechanism, not just advice
4. **Commentary Between Steps** — One-sentence personal notes breaking the mechanical rhythm
5. **Substitution + Compensation + Reassurance** — What to use, how to adjust, and "it'll still be good"
6. **Standalone Wisdom Lines** — Single sentences of culinary truth between sections, no heading
7. **Scene Closer** — Last paragraph paints the table, never gives a command

## The Forbidden Patterns (AI Tells)

| Forbidden | Why it's detectable |
|---|---|
| "This recipe is perfect for..." as first sentence | No human food blogger opens this way |
| "Enjoy!" / "Bon appétit!" as last word | Zero human food blogs end this way |
| "Whether you're a beginner or experienced..." | Generic audience address = AI pattern |
| "Let's dive in!" / "So without further ado..." | Filler transition no human writes |
| "The best part? It only takes X minutes!" | Forced enthusiasm format |
| "So grab your [ingredient] and let's get started!" | Call-to-action no human uses in a recipe post |
