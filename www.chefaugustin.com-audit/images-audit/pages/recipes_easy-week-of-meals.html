Instructions

  1. 11

    Heat olive oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add ground beef in a single layer and cook undisturbed for 2 minutes. Break up with a wooden spoon and cook, stirring occasionally, until no pink remains and an instant-read thermometer registers 160°F / 71°C (USDA minimum for ground beef). Remove beef with a slotted spoon, leaving fat and browned bits behind.

  2. 22

    Reduce heat to medium. Add diced onion and a pinch of salt; cook, stirring, until translucent, about 4 minutes. Add minced garlic and smoked paprika; stir for 30 seconds until fragrant until the internal temperature reaches 145°F / 63°C on an instant-read thermometer.

  3. 33

    Pour in crushed tomatoes and scrape up browned bits with a spoon. Let bubble for 1 minute. Add uncooked rice and stir to coat grains in tomato mixture; cook for 30 seconds.

  4. 44

    Return beef and its juices to the skillet. Add water (or reserved pasta water), remaining salt, and black pepper. Stir once, bring to a low boil, then cover and reduce heat to low. Simmer undisturbed for 18 minutes.

  5. 55

    Uncover: rice should be tender and liquid absorbed. Sprinkle frozen peas over the top, re-cover, and let sit off heat for 3 minutes. Fluff with a fork.

  6. 66

    If using eggs, heat butter in a separate small pan over medium heat. Crack in egg, cover, and cook for 2 minutes until white is set and yolk is runny. Serve on top of the skillet rice.

Why This Works

Ground beef and rice have been sharing a pan for centuries across the Mediterranean, Middle East, and Latin America — and for good reason. When you sear the beef first, you create fond: those browned bits that cling to the skillet, rich with glutamate and Maillard byproducts. Deglazing with canned tomatoes lifts that fond into the dish, turning it from "ground meat and starch" into something with depth. The rice then absorbs the tomato liquid, releasing starch that thickens everything into a risotto-like creaminess without stirring. I used a medium-grain rice here because its amylopectin content (around 15%) creates that tender, clingy texture; long-grain won't deliver the same creaminess, though basmati works if you're going for a pilaf-y separation.

When I tested four variations — tomato-only, stock-only, wine + tomato, and a splash of fish sauce — the tomato-only version won for easy lunch recipes simplicity. No stock to open, no measuring, just the pure acidity cutting through the beef's richness. The frozen peas (added at the end) contribute a pop of sweetness and retain their color because they're shocked just before serving.

Chef's Tips & What I've Learned

Chef Augustin's Tip: On pasta water — That cloudy water left after boiling pasta? It's lightly salted, lightly starched, and ideal for cooking rice. I keep a jar in the fridge for 3 days. If you don't have any, plain water works, but the mouthfeel shifts from silky to just tender. The difference is subtle but real.

Chef Augustin's Tip: On batch cooking — This skillet freezes beautifully. Portion into 2-cup containers, cool completely, freeze flat. Reheat in a skillet with a splash of water, or microwave on medium power, stirring halfway. The texture holds because the rice is already saturated.

A good dinner doesn't announce itself. It just waits.

FAQ

Can I use brown rice instead?

Yes, but increase the liquid to 2 cups and the simmer time to 40 minutes. Brown rice has an intact bran layer that requires more moisture and time to soften. I tested this twice: the flavor is nuttier, the texture chewier. Just don't lift the lid early or you'll lose steam.

How long does this keep in the fridge?

Four days, airtight. Reheat gently. The rice may dry slightly — add a tablespoon of water per cup when reheating.

Can I use fresh tomatoes instead of canned?

Sure: 2 cups diced fresh tomatoes with their juices, plus an extra ¼ cup water. Canned tomatoes have a concentrated sweetness from processing; fresh will be brighter, slightly more acidic. I'd add a teaspoon of honey to round it out.

My beef didn't brown — what went wrong?

Overcrowding. Give the meat room to sear. If your skillet isn't at least 12 inches wide, cook the beef in two batches. Also, pat it dry with a paper towel before adding; surface moisture steams rather than sears.

Can I make this dairy-free?

It already is. If adding cheese for serving, a crumbled sharp cheddar is lovely.

Is this suitable for meal prep?

Absolutely. It's designed for it. Divide into containers with a vegetable side (steamed broccoli, roasted carrots) and you have 4 servings ready to reheat. My own Monday often starts with a container of this and a hard-boiled egg.

A warm skillet, the soft clink of a fork against it, and a quiet kitchen at the end of the day. That's all I'm really offering.
