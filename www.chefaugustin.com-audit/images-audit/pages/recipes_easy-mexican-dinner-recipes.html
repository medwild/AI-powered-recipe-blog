Easy Mexican Dinner for Two: One-Pan Chicken Enchilada Skillet

Ingredients

  • boneless skinless chicken breasts (about 12 oz / 340g total), patted dry2
  • olive oil1 tablespoon (15ml)
  • Diamond Crystal kosher salt, divided1 teaspoon
  • black pepper1/2 teaspoon
  • chili powder2 tablespoons (16g)
  • ground cumin1 teaspoon (2g)
  • garlic, minced3 cloves
  • yellow onion (about 100g), diced1/2 medium
  • crushed tomatoes, fire-roasted if available1 can (14.5 oz / 411g)
  • corn tortillas, torn into bite-size pieces4
  • frozen corn, unthawed1/2 cup (75g)
  • shredded Monterey Jack or cheddar, plus more to taste3/4 cup (85g)
  • water or chicken broth, as needed to loosen sauce1/4 cup (60ml)
  • lime, cut into wedges, for serving1
  • fresh cilantro, roughly chopped, for garnish2 tablespoons (8g)

Instructions

  1. 11

    Pat the chicken breasts completely dry and season both sides with 3/4 teaspoon kosher salt and the black pepper. Heat olive oil in a cast iron or heavy skillet over medium-high until shimmering. Sear the chicken undisturbed until a deep golden crust forms and it releases from the pan on its own, about 3 minutes per side. Remove to a plate — it will not yet be cooked through.

  2. 22

    Lower heat to medium. Add the diced onion to the same pan and cook until softened, about 3 minutes. Push to the side, add the minced garlic, chili powder, and cumin, and bloom in the residual oil for 30 seconds until fragrant and nutty — do not let the spices scorch or they turn bitter.

  3. 33

    Pour in the crushed tomatoes and the remaining 1/4 teaspoon salt, scraping up every browned bit from the bottom of the pan. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 5 minutes to thicken slightly. Taste and add a pinch of sugar if the tomatoes are sharp.

  4. 44

    Slice the seared chicken into strips and nestle it into the sauce along with any resting juices. Simmer, spooning sauce over the top, until the chicken reaches an internal temperature of 165°F / 74°C on an instant-read thermometer.

  5. 55

    Fold the torn corn tortillas and frozen corn directly into the sauce. Add a splash of water or broth if the skillet looks dry. Stir gently until the tortillas soften and absorb the sauce, about 2 minutes.

  6. 66

    Scatter the shredded cheese evenly over the top. Cover the skillet and cook until the cheese is fully melted and bubbling, about 2 minutes. Remove from heat, garnish with fresh cilantro, and serve with lime wedges.

Article and tips

Rolling enchiladas on a Tuesday night is a decision you regret halfway through, sauce dripping off your wrist and three tortillas already torn. So I stopped rolling. This deconstructed enchilada skillet gives you every bit of the melty, saucy, deeply spiced payoff — layered instead of rolled, finished in easy lunch recipes, portioned exactly for two.

Here's the angle the top recipes miss: they all lean on canned enchilada sauce, and canned enchilada sauce tastes like the can. The whole dish is only as good as that sauce. So I build a five-minute chile sauce from a spoonful of pantry chili powder bloomed in oil and a can of crushed tomatoes. It costs nothing extra and it's the difference between "fine" and "why does this taste like a restaurant."

Why This Works

The secret is blooming. When you toast chili powder, cumin, and garlic in hot oil before adding any liquid, the fat-soluble flavor compounds in those spices — capsaicin, the aromatic oils — dissolve into the oil and spread through the entire dish. Dump chili powder into liquid and it stays dusty and one-dimensional; bloom it first and it goes savory and round.

Searing the easy healthy dinner recipes romantic dinner ideas for two for two matters just as much. I've made this both ways — raw chicken simmered straight in sauce versus seared first — and the seared version wins by a wide margin. Searing triggers the Maillard reaction, the browning that creates hundreds of new savory flavor compounds on the surface of the meat. Those browned bits then deglaze into the sauce when the tomatoes hit the hot pan. You're not just cooking chicken; you're building a base.

Corn tortillas, torn and folded into the sauce, do something flour tortillas can't: they absorb liquid while holding their structure, thickening the skillet into that signature saucy-yet-substantial enchilada texture. This is comfort engineered into a single pan.

What Most Recipes Get Wrong

They skip the bloom. Nearly every 30-minute Mexican dinner online tells you to add chili powder to a simmering liquid, and that's the single biggest reason home enchilada sauce tastes flat and dusty. Spices are not salt — they don't dissolve, they infuse, and they only infuse into fat. Thirty seconds of blooming in hot oil before the tomatoes go in is the entire game. The second miss: crowding the pan so the chicken steams gray instead of searing brown. In a skillet romantic dinner for two at home you have room — use it, and give each piece space.

Chef's Tips & What I've Learned

Chef Augustin's Tip: Taste your sauce before the cheese goes on. Canned tomatoes vary wildly in acidity — a pinch of sugar rounds out a sharp batch, and it works because sugar counteracts the perception of acid on your palate.

Chef Augustin's Tip: Cast iron over stainless here. I've tested both, and cast iron holds roughly four times more heat, so it recovers instantly when the cold chicken hits and gives you a far better sear.

A dish made from the pantry on a tired Tuesday is still a dish made with care — the two are not opposites.

How do you make an easy Mexican dinner for two?

Start with a single skillet. Sear seasoned chicken breast to 165°F / 74°C, remove it, then bloom chili powder, cumin, and garlic in the same pan for 30 seconds. Deglaze with a can of crushed tomatoes, simmer five minutes, return the sliced chicken, fold in torn corn tortillas and frozen corn, top with cheese, and melt. Total time is about 35 minutes and it uses one pan, which means one thing to wash.

What ingredients do I need for an enchilada skillet?

The core is chicken breast, canned crushed tomatoes, chili powder, cumin, garlic, onion, corn tortillas, and shredded cheese, plus olive oil for searing. Frozen corn and a squeeze of lime round it out. That's roughly nine pantry-and-freezer staples for two servings. If you keep a stocked pantry, you likely already have most of this without a shopping trip.

Can you freeze this enchilada skillet?

Yes, but freeze it before adding the cheese for best results. The tomato-chicken base freezes well in an airtight container for up to 3 months. Corn tortillas soften on thawing, so if you plan to freeze, hold the tortillas and add fresh ones when reheating. Thaw overnight in the fridge, reheat in a skillet over medium, then top with cheese and melt.

How long does it last in the fridge?

Stored in an airtight container, this skillet keeps for 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator, following USDA guidance for cooked poultry. The tortillas will absorb more sauce over time and soften further — I actually like day-two texture. Reheat gently in a covered skillet with a splash of water to loosen the sauce, or microwave in 45-second bursts until it reaches 165°F / 74°C throughout.

What should I serve with a Mexican dinner skillet?

Keep the sides light since the skillet is rich. A quick shredded cabbage slaw with lime cuts the richness perfectly, or cilantro-lime rice on the side stretches it to feed a hungry pair. For two, I usually just add sliced avocado, a scatter of fresh cilantro, and lime wedges — bright, acidic, and cooling against the warm spice.

Set the skillet down in the middle of the table with two forks, a dish of lime wedges beside it, and let the steam do the talking while you both dig straight in from the pan.
