Small-Batch Edible Cookie Dough for Two

Servings

2

Ingredients

  • all-purpose flour, heat-treated½ cup (60g)
  • unsalted butter, softened to room temperature3 tablespoons (42g)
  • packed dark brown sugar3 tablespoons (38g)
  • granulated sugar2 tablespoons (25g)
  • Diamond Crystal kosher salt⅛ teaspoon
  • pure vanilla extract½ teaspoon
  • whole milk1 tablespoon (15ml)
  • mini semisweet chocolate chips3 tablespoons (30g)
Tips & FAQSave

Instructions

  1. 11

    Heat-treat the flour: Spread flour on a microwave-safe plate. Microwave in 30-second bursts, stirring, until an instant-read thermometer reads 165°F / 74°C. Let cool completely.

  2. 22

    Cream the butter and both sugars together in a small bowl using a fork or hand mixer until mixture resembles damp sand and clumps when pressed, about 2 minutes.

  3. 33

    Add vanilla extract and whole milk; beat until pale and fluffy, about 30 seconds.

  4. 44

    Fold in the cooled, heat-treated flour and salt with a spatula just until no dry streaks remain.

  5. 55

    Gently fold in the mini chocolate chips until evenly distributed. Divide dough into two ramekins. For firmer texture, chill 10 minutes before serving.

Article and tips

Most edible cookie dough recipes are built for a crowd — enough to fill a mixing bowl and then some. But when it's just you and someone you love on a Tuesday night, you don't need a vat of raw dough. You need two ramekins, a spoon, and exactly six minutes. This is the recipe I reach for when the sweet craving hits after dinner and I want something fast, safe, and scaled to our small household. No eggs, no waiting, and a 60-second flour safety step that makes it completely worry-free.

A lonely scoop of dough on the counter is a quiet promise.

Why This Works

I've tested this with both melted and softened butter. Softened butter — beaten with the sugars until the mixture looks like damp sand and clings together when pressed — gives the dough that dense, fudgy chew you expect from cookie dough without ever hitting the oven. Melted butter makes it greasy and one-dimensional. The real secret, though, is what's missing: eggs. Leaving them out keeps the dough safe to eat straight from the bowl and gives it a pure butter-sugar-vanilla flavor. A splash of whole milk binds everything just enough to hold a scoop without turning the dough cakey.

And then there's the flour. Raw flour can carry E. coli — a fact many recipes gloss over. Heating it to 165°F / 74°C neutralizes the risk. I microwave it on a plate in 30-second bursts, stirring between, until an instant-read thermometer confirms the temperature. It takes about 60 seconds total. The flour stays pale and powdery; you won't taste the difference, only the safety.

What Most Recipes Get Wrong

Most recipes treat edible cookie dough like a half-finished batch of drop cookies — creaming eggs into the mix when they aren't needed, then suggesting you eat it raw anyway. An egg gives structure during baking, but without the oven, all it contributes is a faintly custardy note and a salmonella gamble I'd rather skip. And the quantities? A typical recipe yields three cups, far more than two people want in one sitting. I've designed this one to make exactly enough romantic dinner ideas for two for two generous ramekins — about one packed cup total. No leftovers, no waste.

Chef's Tips & What I've Learned

  • No mixer? No problem. A fork, a small bowl, and two minutes of elbow grease will cream the butter and sugars perfectly. I've made this in a hotel room with nothing but takeout utensils. The key is to push the butter against the side of the bowl repeatedly until the sugars dissolve.
  • Salt the top, not the bowl. A single flake of Maldon sea salt on each serving creates a crunchy, savory pop against the sweet dough. It's a tiny detail that makes the ramekin feel like a dessert, not a snack.
  • Chef Augustin's Tip: If you like the taste of unbaked cookie dough but want something scoopable, leave the dough at room temperature. If you want the firm texture of a fresh-baked cookie, chill it for 30 minutes. The butter sets up, and the chocolate chips snap when you bite.
  • This dough freezes beautifully. Press it into a disc, wrap tightly, and freeze for up to a month. Let it sit at room temperature for 15 minutes before eating — it softens to the same fudgy consistency as day one.

FAQ

Can I skip heat-treating the flour?

You can, but I wouldn't. Raw flour is an agricultural product and can carry E. coli bacteria that cause foodborne illness. Heating it to 165°F / 74°C, as the FDA recommends, eliminates that risk. I microwave it on a plate because the thin layer heats evenly and cools fast. Without this step, edible cookie dough isn't truly safe, no matter how many food blogs tell you otherwise.

Why no eggs in this edible cookie dough?

Eggs provide structure when baked, but in a no-bake dough they add a raw, sliminess and a salmonella risk I'm not willing to take. Pasteurized eggs are an option, but they still leave a faint eggy taste that competes with the butter-vanilla flavor. Leaving them out entirely lets the sugar and butter shine while keeping the dough safe to eat with zero worry. The milk and creaming method give you all the richness you need.

My dough is crumbly — how do I fix it?

Crumbliness usually means the butter wasn't creamed long enough, or the flour wasn't cooled before adding. If the flour is still warm, it melts the butter on contact and breaks the emulsion. Let the flour cool completely, then knead the dough with your fingertips for 20 seconds. If it's still dry, add milk ½ teaspoon at a time until it holds together.

Can I use regular-sized chocolate chips?

You can, but mini chips distribute far more evenly in such a mac and cheese for 2 for 2 for 2 for 2](/recipes/mac-and-cheese-for-2). Regular chips tend to cluster in the middle and leave the edges bare. If regular chips are all you have, chop them roughly with a chef's knife — aim for pieces about the size of mini chips. This extra step takes 15 seconds and transforms the texture.

How long does this edible cookie dough last?

Stored in a covered container in the fridge, it keeps for up to 3 days. The flavor actually deepens overnight as the brown sugar and vanilla meld. Let it sit at room temperature for 10 minutes to soften before eating. For longer storage, freeze it (see Chef's Tips above). The dough won't spoil quickly because there are no eggs, but the chocolate chips can bloom — develop a white film — if left too long; still safe, but less pretty.
