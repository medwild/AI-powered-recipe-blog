Garlic Butter Chicken Bites with Blistered Tomatoes and Sautéed Spinach

Prep

Servings

Crispy, golden chicken bites in a garlic butter pan sauce with burst cherry tomatoes and wilted spinach. A 25-minute, one-skillet dinner for two that tastes like it took far longer.

By Chef Augustin LefèvreJuly 29, 2026· Updated August 5, 2026

Ingredients

  • boneless, skinless chicken breasts, cut into 1-inch chunks1 pound (450g)
  • Diamond Crystal kosher salt1 teaspoon
  • freshly ground black pepper1/2 teaspoon
  • olive oil2 tablespoons
  • unsalted butter, divided2 tablespoons
  • garlic, thinly sliced4 cloves
  • red pepper flakes1/4 teaspoon
  • cherry tomatoes, halved1 cup (150g)
  • fresh spinach, roughly chopped4 cups (120g)
  • fresh lemon juice1 tablespoon
  • grated Parmesan cheese (optional)2 tablespoons
  • Fresh parsley, chopped, for garnish
  • Cooked rice or pasta, for serving
Tips & FAQSave

Instructions

  1. 11

    Pat chicken chunks completely dry with paper towels. Toss with salt and pepper.

  2. 22

    Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high until shimmering. Add chicken in a single layer, not touching. Sear without moving for 3–4 minutes until deeply golden on one side.

  3. 33

    Flip chicken pieces and sear the second side for 2–3 minutes more. The internal temperature should reach 165°F / 74°C at the thickest part. Transfer chicken to a plate.

  4. 44

    Reduce heat to medium. Add 1 tablespoon of the butter, garlic, and red pepper flakes. Stir for 30 seconds until fragrant but not browned.

  5. 55

    Add halved cherry tomatoes and a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring occasionally, for 2–3 minutes until they begin to release their juices and blister slightly.

  6. 66

    Add the spinach in handfuls, stirring just until wilted, about 1 minute. Return the chicken and any accumulated juices to the skillet.

  7. 77

    Swirl in the remaining 1 tablespoon butter and the lemon juice. Let everything come together for 30 seconds, then remove from heat. Sprinkle with Parmesan if using. Serve over rice or pasta, garnished with parsley.

Why This Works

The magic here is the trio of a proper sear, the fond it leaves behind, and the butter-sautéed vegetables that lift it all. When you drop cold, wet chicken into a hot pan, you steam it, not sear it. But when you pat the chicken dry and let it sit undisturbed in shimmering olive oil, the surface hits 300°F almost immediately. That's where the Maillard reaction creates the deep, golden crust that makes you reach for another bite before you've even finished the first.

Then the deglazing step — scraping up those brown bits with garlic and bursting tomatoes — folds that flavor into the sauce. No stock needed; the tomatoes release enough liquid, and the spinach's own moisture steam-sautés it right in the pan. I've tested this with both olive oil alone and the butter finish. The butter at the end (never at the beginning — it would burn) gives the sauce a velvet texture that coats every chunk without hiding the healthy dinner recipes for 2 for 2 for 2's crisp edges.

A note on internal temperature: chicken breast dries out above 165°F / 74°C faster than you can say "oops." I pull it at that exact point. A rested piece will still glisten inside, not weep onto the plate.

What Most Recipes Get Wrong

Top search results for "dinner for two" mostly give you lists — dozens of links with a photo and a one-sentence description. They don't stop to teach. The single biggest mistake I see: starting with wet chicken in a not-hot-enough pan. The second mistake: adding tomatoes before the garlic has had 30 seconds alone with the butter, which mutes the garlic's punch. The third: never mentioning that a butter-sauced dish needs a hit of acid — lemon juice or even a splash of vinegar — to taste complete. These three fixes transform the dish from "fine" to "why have I been settling?"

Chef's Tips & What I've Learned

Chef Augustin's Tip: Patience with the sear is the best ingredient you already own. Three minutes without poking at the easy 4 ingredient chicken breast recipes gets you a crust that tastes like you used a grill pan, even in a smooth skillet.

A pan sauce is nothing more than fond, fat, and a little liquid coaxed into a quick emulsion. When you swirl that cold butter off the heat, you're creating tiny fat droplets suspended in the tomato juices. It's the same principle behind a classic beurre blanc, just done in a minute with ingredients you already had.

Chef Augustin's Tip: Always taste your sauce after the lemon juice. If it still feels flat, you need more salt — not more butter. Salt makes flavor bloom; butter just carries it.

FAQ

Can I use chicken thighs instead?

Yes, and I often do. Boneless, skinless thighs need an extra 1–2 minutes per side because they contain more moisture, but they're far more forgiving if you overshoot the temperature a few degrees — they'll stay juicy through 175°F / 79°C. Cut them the same 1-inch size and pat them even more thoroughly; thighs hold more surface water.

How do I know the pan is hot enough without a thermometer?

Flick a single drop of water into the dry skillet. If it skitters across the surface and evaporates in 2–3 seconds, you're at searing temperature. If it sits and bubbles lazily, keep heating.

Can I make this dairy-free?

Absolutely. Skip the butter and use an extra tablespoon of olive oil for the garlic step, then finish with a spoonful of a good, fruit-forward extra-virgin olive oil instead of the final butter addition. The sauce won't have the same velvet body, but the dish still works beautifully. The Parmesan is optional — leave it out for dairy-free.

What's the best way to reheat leftovers?

The microwave turns the chicken rubbery. Instead, reheat gently in a skillet over medium-low with a tablespoon of water or chicken stock. Cover with a lid for 2–3 minutes; the steam revives the sauce without drying the meat. Internal temperature only needs to reach 165°F / 74°C again, but that happens quickly.

Can I add mushrooms or bell peppers?

Mushrooms are wonderful: slice them and sauté in the skillet after removing the chicken, before the garlic. They need 4–5 minutes to release their water and brown, so plan for an extra 5 minutes of cooking. Bell peppers soften faster — add them alongside the tomatoes. Both work, and the one-skillet formula stays intact.
